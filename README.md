# Philosophy
Bittersweet Supplement is a Minecraft datapack project focused on slowing down progression while giving some unknown or obscure mechanics and items more uses and time in the spotlight. I also aim to reward exploration and remove the need for redstone farms and mob grinders.

# Changes
## Enchanting and Repairing
- Experience is removed; enchanting has no experience cost, and repairing only has its material cost.
- Anvils no longer break or deteriorate when used.
- The enchantment table has been removed.
- Inspired by Raspberry Flavoured, enchantments are now obtained via multi-enchanted items. For Bittersweet, these are **boons**.
- Boons have 2-3 enchantments on average, with only one of the above being applicable to any given item.
- Each boon is crafted with an **Empty Boon**. This locks enchanting behind gold, rather than diamond.
- Enchantment levels no longer exist, with each enchantment starting and ending at level 1.
- Some enchantments are exclusive to specific items.
- Many enchantments have been tweaked, reworked, or replaced.

## Food and Furnaces
- Hunger no longer exists.
- Food heals you directly, with nuance existing via the three main variations of food: total health gain, health gain speed, and consumption speed.
- Alongside this, I have taken inspiration from _The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom_ via its food system. Obviously, food heals you. But certain foods may also grant buffs.
- The furnace is crafted with bricks, unable to cook food, and yields only nuggets from iron and gold.
- Furthermore, the blast furnace—which requires deepslate—is required for processing later tiers of materials.
- Potions are now crafted at a crafting table, and the brewing stand is uncraftable.

## Progression
- The early game has been lengthened, and so has the day-night cycle.
- Campfires, flint, and copper all become more relevant—necessary for progress.
- Armor is weaker across the board, and set bonuses now exist.
- Durability is increased across the board, especially for armor.
- To compensate, Mending and Unbreaking do not exist.
- Copper is now required to mine iron ore.
- Chainmail is craftable.
- Gold is now a much more useful material, and a proper middle-stage between iron and diamond.
- Gold is able to be repaired on-the-go, and has more durability while still being frail. Its fast nature extends to weaponry now, too.
- Diamond and Adamantine (netherite) are sidegrades to eachother, both smithed from gold equipment.
- Ancient debris is now more common, and diamond is less common.
- Diamond retains gold's speed, while adding durability and damage.
- Adamantine does away with gold's speed, being extremely durable as well as having more damage output.

## Balancing
- The mace, trident, and elytra are now moved to a new tier of equipment: Mythical gear and weapons.
- The mace is now able to create wind bursts on demand, with a cooldown.
- The mace is now able to mine without losing durability, and mine in a 3x3 radius at the cost of durability.
- The trident attacks like a spear and comes with reach, more damage, and the loyalty enchantment.
- Axes now all share a 0.8 second attack cooldown, with their damages adjusted accordingly.
- Dying removes a heart from your maximum. Every 15 minutes, you will regain lost hearts. It is advised, but not required, to take this time to prepare more adequately.
- Many crafting recipes have been tweaked, either making progression harder or life easier for builders.
- Many mob drops have been tweaked to reduce inventory bloating, offer more generous rewards, or remove unnecessarily low drop chances.

## Mobs
- Almost every mob and boss has been tweaked or fully reworked!
- Mobs no longer drop their equipment.
- Mob equipment is directly correlated to the most progressed player.
- Every boss drops an **Abyssal Star** (nether star), or **Abyssal Shards**.
- Generic cobblestone dungeons have been reworked:
- Dungeon spawners now have a limited # of mobs that they can spawn, doing so in waves akin to their trial counterparts.
- Spawners drop an Abyssal Shard.
- Phantoms have been removed, with a new phantom boss being added.
- Sleeping consecutive nights has consequences.
- Villagers have been removed, hunted to extinction by illagers.
- The Wandering Trader is a masked entity, returning even from death time-to-time.
- The Wandering Trader offers exotic goods such as rare saplings, maps, and hard-to-obtain items and blocks in exchange for emeralds.
- Snow golems are able to defend themselves at close range by freezing hostile mobs around them, but their life force drains gradually over time, even in cold biomes.
  
# New Content
## General
- Pulaskis (Shovel+Axe) and Mattocks (Pickaxe+Hoe) are made with one piece of the next tier in progression. They are more durable, but slower than their non multitool counterparts.
- New tier-exclusive weapons have been added to serve specific purposes for their stages of the game.
- New weapons to fit specific gaps in equipment niches.
- 3+ New structures!
- Many new enchantments!
## Bronze
- Bronze is on-par with diamond and netherite, found in the nether uncommonly.
- Bronze can be used to upgrade tier-exclusives to end-game level.
- Bronze armor grants no protection, only a damage boost.
- The new Bronze Gauntlet acts as an all-in-one mining tool.
## Verdum
- Verdum is a living material used to create three items; a quarterstaff, and both multitools.
- Verdum comes with the **Verdure** enchantment, which inflicts poison and mends itself in sunlight.
- The Verdum Quarterstaff can block attacks, storing damage to dish out during critical hits.
## Crossbows
- The crossbow has been split into three, each having similar damage output and different ways of dishing it out.
- Iron Crossbow: Comes with Sharpshooter (power), shooting very fast, gravity-ignoring arrows.
- Gilded Crossbow: Comes with Quick Charge, which has been buffed to shoot even faster.
- Chambered Crossbow: Comes with Overload, loading up to 5 arrows at once.
